Interiors rules and use zones

Furniture needs room round it: beside a bed to get in and out, in front of a dresser to open its drawers, round a table to pull the chairs back. Riviera keeps the rules for that room as rule packs, each from one standard or guide. It shows each piece's use zones as it is placed. Check Rules then lists what stands in them. The sample An accessible hotel guest room shows the tools.

Rule packs

Choose packs in Manage › Model Properties › Interiors rules. With the box ticked, they follow the Accessibility rule set: the 2010 ADA Standards bring in the ADA interiors pack. Untick it to choose packs yourself. Two packs ship:

  • 2010 ADA Standards: furniture in accessible rooms. A 30 × 48 in (762 × 1219 mm) clear floor space for a parallel approach along each side of a bed (§806.2.3).
  • Interior planning practice. Practice from planning guides, reported as notes: 36 in round a dining table's edge, 30 in in front of a dresser, 24 in at the foot of a bed.

Each rule carries its clause, its source and how sure the reading is. A finding from guidance, or from a rule marked advisory, is a note, never an error.

Which furniture a rule applies to

A rule chooses furniture by its IFC type: BED, TABLE, DESK, CHAIR, SOFA, SHELF or FILECABINET. A piece with none of these is USERDEFINED, and then by its Kind: Dresser, Nightstand, Treadmill. Select a placed piece and set both in the Properties palette's Type rows. Every copy of the type follows them.

Use zones

Use zones are virtual volumes, in green like bathroom clear spaces. Show or hide them with View › Virtual Volumes. A piece's front is its −y side as modelled: a bed's foot, a dresser's drawers. A zone is set out across the piece, from its centre or its left or right edge, and from its front or back. Each zone runs from the floor to the rule's height (1.0 m unless the rule says otherwise). The zones move and turn with the piece. They never change its geometry.

Floors, rugs and other floor coverings, accessories, and other pieces' zones may share a zone. Anything else in it is reported.

Check Rules

View › Coordination › Check Rules lists each finding. A finding gives the rule, the pack and its edition, and what stands in the zone. Errors come first. Pick a finding to select and frame the piece and what is in its way. Check Overlaps includes use zones too.

The list's first row, Save Report…, keeps the findings as a file: HTML, Markdown, CSV or JSON, on this computer or in a FileEngine folder. Manage › Settings › Save Report does the same for any check.

Room layouts

Architecture › Build › Room Layout furnishes a room from a template. The templates are bedrooms (queen, king, two twins, a UK double), a living room, dining rooms for four and six, a home office, a private office and a meeting room for eight. Click inside a room closed by walls. The main piece (a bed's head, a sofa's back, a desk) goes against one of the longer walls, and the rest round it.

The layout fits only when every piece, and every use zone the rule packs give it, stays in the room and clear of the other pieces. Otherwise nothing is placed, and Riviera says how much longer or deeper the room must be. Choosing Replace it clears an earlier layout in the same room first. The sample A furnished apartment lays out four rooms. (interior.layouts, interior.layout)

Decorative lighting

The Interiors library's Lighting folder holds chandeliers, a pendant, a wall sconce and lamps. Select a wall, or a table or counter, and choose Architecture › Build › Decorative Lighting:

  • on a wall, sconces along its room side, one every 8 ft, centred at 63 in;
  • over a table, a chandelier sized from the room and the table (the room's width plus length in feet gives its largest diameter in inches; over a table, ⅔ of the table's length), its bottom 32 in over the table;
  • pendants in a row along a table or counter, 27 in apart, 33 in over it.

Check Rules then applies the 2010 ADA Standards: 80 in headroom under anything hanging over a walkway (§307.4), and no more than 4 in out from a wall between 27 and 80 in up (§307.2). Planning practice's heights over tables come as notes. (lighting.chandelier, lighting.pendants, lighting.sconces)

Cabinet runs and countertops

Choose Architecture › Build › Cabinet Run, pick Base, Wall or Tall units and the sizes, then click the run's start and end on a wall's face in a plan. The run is filled with standard widths so that the least is left, and a filler takes the rest:

  • US (the IBC or IRC): base units 24 in deep and 34½ in high on a 4 in toe kick, in 36 in units and 9–48 in widths in 3 in steps; wall units 12 × 30 in from 54 in; tall units 84 in;
  • EU: 600 mm modules, base units 870 mm high on a 150 mm plinth, wall units from 1460 mm.

The units face away from the wall. They are Casework, typed by width and built in, and the FF&E schedule counts them. Countertop then tops each run of base units, or the selected units' runs: 1½ in thick, 1 in past the fronts (20 mm in the EU), its surface at 36 in. The sample A galley kitchen has two runs, wall units and a pantry. Corners, sink and cooktop cut-outs and backsplashes are not made yet. (cabinet.run, cabinet.runs, countertop.from_runs)

Counters

Architecture › Build › Counter lays a counter along a wall the same way, clicked from start to end: a transaction or bar counter at 42 in, a service counter at 36 in, or a work surface at 29 in, open below. Choose an accessible portion at either end and it is lowered to 36 in (2010 ADA Standards §904.4):

  • for a parallel approach, 36 in long, with a 48 × 30 in clear floor space along it;
  • for a forward approach, 30 in long and open below, with a 30 × 48 in space in front and knee clearance under it, 27 in high and 17 in deep (§306).

Check Rules reports a portion that is too high or too short, and anything in its spaces. The sample Service counters has a teller counter, a reception desk and a writing surface. (counter.run)

Closets

Choose Architecture › Build › Closet and click the closet's back wall face from one side to the other. The rods are centred in a 24 in deep closet and a 12 in shelf goes on the wall:

  • single: a rod at 66 in under a shelf at 68 in;
  • double hang: rods at 40 and 80 in under a shelf at 84 in;
  • accessible: a rod at 42 in and a shelf at 46 in. Reaching sideways over the closet's depth, the 2010 ADA Standards allow 46 in at most (§308.3.2), and Check Rules says so if they are raised.

The sample Closets has one of each. Doors and walk-ins are not made yet. (closet.create)

Hotel guest rooms

Architecture › Build › Room Layout has four guest rooms: a king (3.6 × 4.6 m inside), two queens (4.0 × 4.6 m), an accessible king (3.6 × 5.0 m, its bed keeping a 30 × 48 in space each side) and a junior suite (5.5 × 4.6 m). Each marks its room as a guest room, placing the room when there is none. A standard room's pieces are marked not accessible, so only the accessible room has the ADA's bed spaces. The bathroom is its own room: lay it out with Bathroom Layout.

Select rooms and choose Architecture › Build › Guest Room to set their class, beds and features: mobility, mobility with a roll-in shower, and communication (visible alarms and notification, §806.3).

View › Coordination › Room Matrix counts the guest rooms by floor and by class and checks them:

  • 2010 ADA Standards: Table 224.2 (mobility rooms without and with roll-in showers), Table 224.4 (communication rooms) and §224.5. At least one mobility room must have communication features too, but only a tenth of the mobility rooms required may count toward Table 224.4: in a small hotel, none. A class with no accessible room, while there are enough to go round, is a note.
  • Approved Document M vol. 2: one wheelchair-accessible bedroom per 20 (§4.24).

The building code chooses the standard (England's code, Approved Document M; otherwise the ADA), or pick one. For an alteration, enter the rooms altered or added as the rooms counted. The reading of the tables is provisional (EXPERT_REVIEW.md E-109). The sample A hotel floor has six rooms that meet the counts. (hotel.set_room, hotel.room_matrix)

Gondola runs and aisles

Choose Architecture › Build › Gondola Run, then click the run's start and end:

  • island: its centre line. Shelves face both ways, 72 in high on 16 in decks (35¾ in deep), with an end cap at each end as wide as the island;
  • wall: its back line on the wall's face. Shelves face away from the wall, 84 in high.

The run is filled with 48 in sections, or the width chosen, using 36, 30 and 24 in sections for the rest (the fixture makers' sizes). View › Coordination › Check Rules then reports any aisle that is too narrow:

  • 2010 ADA Standards: 36 in clear along each face (§403.5.1), and 48 in beyond each end cap to turn round the run (§403.5.2);
  • IBC Group M: 44 in, a public aisle no narrower than a corridor. Older editions asked 36 in with shelves on one side only; the pack is in use under the IBC;
  • store practice: 6 ft for two carts, as a note, when Store planning practice is ticked under Manage › Model Properties › Retail rules.

The values are provisional (EXPERT_REVIEW.md E-110). The sample Store aisles has a wall run and three islands 2 m apart. (retail.run, retail.runs)

Store checks

Select a store's rooms and choose Architecture › Build › Store Area to mark them as the sales floor or stock rooms. View › Coordination › Check Store then gives:

  • the occupant load: each room's area at 60 ft² a person on the sales floor and 300 ft² in stock rooms (IBC Table 1004.5), by level and in all;
  • sprinkler clearance: every gondola, end cap and pallet rack at least 18 in below the deflector of a sprinkler over it, 36 in below an ESFR head (an error);
  • high-piled storage: anything over 12 ft, which brings the fire code's chapter 32 rules (a note);
  • anchoring: gondolas over 96 in, wall gondolas over their unanchored height (78 in on 16 in shelves), and pallet racks 8 ft or more high on the sales floor (notes).

Pick a finding to select and frame the fixture. The values are provisional (EXPERT_REVIEW.md E-111). The sample Store aisles holds 31 people. (retail.set_area, retail.check)

Checkouts

Choose Architecture › Build › Checkout Bank, pick the lanes and their handing, then click the line their exit ends stand on and a point on the sales floor. The lanes go at the pitch (84 in: a 48 in stand and a 36 in aisle), or in tandem pairs. As many lanes as the 2010 ADA Standards' Table 227.2 asks are made accessible, spread across the bank: one of 1–4, two of 5–8, three of 9–15, then 20 % of those over 15. Check Rules keeps 36 in clear beside each (§904.3.1) and, with Store planning practice, each lane's queue 3 m beyond its in-feed.

View › Coordination › Check Checkouts counts every function (checkout, express, pharmacy; each self-checkout bank on its own). Under 5,000 ft² of selling space one accessible lane is enough. Checkout Lanes Estimate gives the staffed lanes a peak hour needs from a queueing model: an estimate, not a requirement. The values are provisional (EXPERT_REVIEW.md E-112). The sample A store front has three banks. (frontend.layout, checkout.check, checkout.lanes_estimate)

Self-checkout pods

Choose Architecture › Build › Self-checkout Pod, pick the units, how many and the courtyard (11 ft: NCR leaves 10–12 ft). Then click the pod's open end and a point down the pod. The units stand in two rows facing each other, with an attendant station at the closed end facing the entry. A shared queue reaches 3 m beyond the entry. The pod is a self-checkout bank of its own: the accessible units Table 227.2 asks are spread over both rows.

The 2010 ADA Standards have no self-checkout section, and the Access Board's 2022 proposal is not a rule. So Riviera checks the accessible units against a profile built from EN 301 549 cl. 8.3 and that proposal, as advice. Select the units and choose Self-checkout Profile to state what the maker gives:

  • the lowest and highest operable part, and how deep the reach is;
  • the screen's height;
  • speech output, a tactile keypad and Braille.

View › Coordination › Check Self-checkouts checks each pod:

  • Practice notes (NCR): the courtyard's width, an attendant station for every 4–8 units with a clear sightline to each screen, the queue kept clear, and 96 in over each unit for its lane light.
  • Advisory findings for each accessible unit: operable parts 380–1220 mm (lower over a deep reach), a 760 × 1220 mm clear floor space, speech, a keypad and Braille. At least one unit in a pod should meet the whole profile.

The values are provisional (EXPERT_REVIEW.md E-113). The sample Self-checkout pods has two pods. (sco.place_pod, sco.set_profile, sco.check)

Gyms

Place equipment from the Riviera Gym Equipment library. Each piece's clear zones show as virtual volumes, and Check Rules reports what stands in them. The equipment makers' clearances are always in use:

  • a treadmill keeps 0.5 m along each side and 2 m behind the belt, where its user steps on;
  • a rower keeps its 2.74 × 1.22 m use area, and a dumbbell rack 0.9 m in front;
  • strength stations keep 0.9 m round them, as a note.

Architecture › Build › Wall Mirror puts a mirror along a wall, from 0.4 to 2.1 m, of safety glazing. View › Coordination › Check Gym checks:

  • with the ADA rule set, one of each type of machine accessible, with a 30 × 48 in clear floor space beside it (§236.1, §1004.1);
  • the lowest thing over each piece: a treadmill needs 2.5 m, a stair climber 2.9 m, a rack 2.7 m, a lifting platform 3.7 m;
  • mirrors in line with a treadmill (Sport England keeps them to the free-weight area), and mirrors without safety glazing.

To fill a room, select it and choose Architecture › Build › Gym Layout. Enter how many of each piece you want. They go in rows from the room's longest wall, each kept clear by its own zones, with a 36 in aisle between rows; anything that does not fit is reported. Studio gives a room's capacity for cycling (1.5 × 2.0 m a bike), mat classes (25 ft² a person) or HIIT (50 ft²) and fills a cycling studio with bikes facing the instructor. Check Gym also gives each room's occupant load at 50 ft² gross.

The values are provisional (EXPERT_REVIEW.md E-114, E-115). The sample A fitness room has a cardio row, a strength area, a cycling studio and a circuit room. (gym.mirror, gym.check, gym.layout, gym.studio)

Accessible and not

The ADA asks only some beds and rooms to have their clear spaces. The accessibility pack's rules apply to every piece unless it is marked not accessible (rules.set_accessible). Mark a bed not accessible when it shares one clear space with the next bed, which §806.2.3 also allows. Practice packs apply to every piece.

Your own packs

A pack is a JSON file following the published schema riviera.rules-1 (MIT, schemas/rules/). rules.add_pack adds one to the project, and it is saved with the model. A pack with a shipped pack's id replaces it, which is how to adopt a local amendment or an office standard. rules.remove_pack brings the shipped one back. The same commands take bathroom and vertical-transport packs and put them where the bathroom and elevator tools read them.

Limits and what to check

  • The bundled Riviera Interiors library's furniture comes typed. Your own imported furniture needs its IFC Type or Kind set before the packs see it.
  • Riviera does not find two beds sharing one clear space; mark one not accessible.
  • Turning spaces, reach ranges and room counts (ADA Table 224.2) are not checked yet.
  • Practice values are from planning guides, not codes. Check them against the brief.

Scripts and agents

Every step is a command, in Lua as riviera.rules.… and through the MCP server:

  • packs: rules.packs, rules.set_packs, rules.add_pack, rules.remove_pack, rules.schema;
  • checks: rules.set_accessible, rules.check;
  • types: mesh_object.set_type with predefined_type or element_type.
