Swimming pools

A swimming pool is one element. Give its outline or pick a template, then its depths: Riviera makes its shell (walls and a sloped base slab), its coping and its water, and cuts the floor round it, all kept up to date as you change it. The sample A swimming pool hall has a 25 m pool with starting platforms, ladders, a lift and a lifeguard chair, and a teaching pool with stairs and a sloped entry; A public pool checked against the codes has stairs, a sloped entry, ladders, a lift and drains, and passes its checks; A backyard pool is a residential pool with corner stairs, a bench, a ladder and an underwater light. Hot tubs, saunas and steam rooms are on the bathrooms page.

Placing one

Choose Architecture › Build › Swimming Pool and a template:

  • Competition 25 m, 50 m and 25 yd: 2.5 m lanes (six by default, eight for 50 m), 30 mm longer for touch pads, 2.0 m deep throughout;
  • Teaching: 12 × 7 m, 0.9 m deep (Sport England's teaching pool);
  • Lap pool, Rectangle by their sizes, and a Wading pool 0.45 m deep.

Click the pool's centre, then a point toward its deep end. Or type an outline as coordinates, starting with the shallow end's wall and going counter-clockwise. The status bar gives its water area, volume and depths.

Depths and slopes

The floor follows stations along the pool's axis, from the shallow end to the deep end: each a distance and the depth below the water there. Between stations the floor is a straight slope; beyond the first and last it is level. Type them as 0:1.0, 7:1.52, 10:2.0. Without them a pool is 1.0 m deep at the shallow end, 5 ft (1.52 m, the break point where the codes' slope limits change) at 60 % of its length and 2.0 m at the deep end. Each segment's slope is given as 1:n; Check Pools judges it against the codes.

Edges, coping and shell

  • Edge: skimmer pools have their water 0.15 m below the deck and a coping 0.3 m wide, overhanging the water by 40 mm (the MAHC's 1–2 in) and 60 mm thick. Deck-level gutter pools have their water at the deck and no coping.
  • Shell: 0.3 m of cast concrete by default, walls outside the outline and a base slab under the floor. The floor on the pool's level is cut round it.

The values are provisional (EXPERT_REVIEW.md E-116).

Stairs, sloped entries and benches

Select the pool and choose Architecture › Build › Pool Entry, then click by the wall where the entry's middle goes. It runs from that edge into the pool and becomes part of it:

  • Stairs, 1.5 m wide: the depth at the wall split into equal risers no higher than 0.25 m, on 12 in (0.305 m) treads.
  • Sloped entry, 1.0 m wide: a 1:12 ramp from the deck to 27 in below the water, the ADA's accessible entry (§1009.3); it stops where the pool does.
  • Underwater bench, 2.0 m long and 0.45 m deep, its seat 0.45 m below the water.

The water's volume is less what they displace. Check Pools counts stairs and sloped entries as entries, a sloped entry as an accessible one, and measures treads, risers, widths, the ramp's slope and depth, and benches. From a script: pool.add_entry, pool.remove_entry, or pool.set with entries.

Equipment

The Riviera Pools library (Insert › Component Libraries) has a pool lift, a three-step ladder, a grab rail, a starting platform, a 1 m diving board, a lifeguard chair, main drain covers and an underwater light. Each faces the water at its front; a ladder's and a light's insertion point is the pool wall's face. Their clear decks show as virtual volumes and Check Rules reports what stands in them: the ADA's 36 × 48 in behind a lift, and the MAHC's 4 ft beside and behind diving boards, lifeguard chairs and lifts. Check Pools counts the ladders and lift as entries and the covers as drains, and does not count the equipment as blocking the deck.

Checking a pool

View › Coordination › Check Pools checks each pool against the pool rule packs in use. Under the IBC these are the ISPSC and the VGB Act's drain rules; with the ADA rule set, the ADA's §242 and §1009. The CDC's Model Aquatic Health Code is a project's choice: tick it in Manage › Model Properties under Pool rules. Each pack checks its own value, so where the codes disagree (1:12 or 1:10 above the break, a 4 ft or 6 ft deck) you see which pack a pool fails.

  • Class: a pool is public, competition, residential or wading, from its template or set with pool.set; each rule names the classes it applies to.
  • Slopes and depths: each segment either side of the 5 ft break, the shallow end, the deepest point, the cove and the coping.
  • Deck: a floor that reaches the width along every edge, with nothing standing in it.
  • Entries: Pool ladder, stairs and recessed steps within 1 m of the edge; the Pool lift, sloped entries and transfer walls as accessible means of entry. A pool wall under 300 ft needs one, a lift or sloped entry; longer, two. The ladder, lift and drains are in the Riviera Pools library.
  • Drains: main drains in the pool, two at least 3 ft apart, or one with a cover larger than 18 × 23 in (unblockable).
  • Bather loads: the MAHC's 20 ft² of water a bather and the IBC's 50 ft² an occupant.

Findings marked secondary came from a checklist or summary, not the code's own text (the ISPSC's public-pool values): check them against your adopted edition.

In IFC

A pool is an IfcElementAssembly (SWIMMINGPOOL) of an IfcWall, an IfcSlab (BASESLAB) and an IfcCovering (COPING). Its water is an IfcSpace (WATER) under the level, with its area and volume in Qto_SpaceBaseQuantities. No IFC version has a pool class, so this is Riviera's mapping.

Limits and what to check

  • Freeform outlines are typed, not yet sketched; profiles are changed from a script.
  • Gutters and infinity-edge basins are recorded but not drawn; the wall–floor cove is a value only; tiling and the membrane are not drawn.
  • Not checked yet: depth markings and NO DIVING markers, the break-point and float lines, barriers and gates, where entries stand along the pool, handrails, and diving envelopes. Recessed steps, swimouts and beach entries are not built yet.

Scripts and agents

Every step is a command, in Lua as riviera.pool.… and through the MCP server: pool.create, pool.set, pool.info, pool.list, pool.add_entry, pool.remove_entry, pool.check; packs are chosen with rules.set_packs.
