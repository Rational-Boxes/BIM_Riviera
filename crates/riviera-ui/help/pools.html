Swimming pools

A swimming pool is one element. Give its outline or pick a template, then its depths: Riviera makes its shell (walls and a sloped base slab), its coping and its water, and cuts the floor round it, all kept up to date as you change it. The sample A swimming pool hall has a 25 m pool with starting platforms, ladders, a lift and a lifeguard chair, and a teaching pool with stairs and a sloped entry; A public pool checked against the codes has stairs, a sloped entry, ladders, a lift and drains, and passes its checks; A backyard pool is a residential pool with corner stairs, a bench, a ladder, an underwater light and a patio receptacle; A rooftop pool puts a hotel pool's weight on its roof slab and checks the terrace's electrics. Hot tubs, saunas and steam rooms are on the bathrooms page.

Placing one

Choose Interiors › Pools › Swimming Pool and a template:

  • Competition 25 m, 50 m and 25 yd: 2.5 m lanes (six by default, eight for 50 m), 30 mm longer for touch pads, 2.0 m deep throughout;
  • Teaching: 12 × 7 m, 0.9 m deep (Sport England's teaching pool);
  • Lap pool, Rectangle by their sizes, and a Wading pool 0.45 m deep.

Click the pool's centre, then a point toward its deep end. Or type an outline as coordinates, starting with the shallow end's wall and going counter-clockwise. The status bar gives its water area, volume and depths.

Depths and slopes

The floor follows stations along the pool's axis, from the shallow end to the deep end: each a distance and the depth below the water there. Between stations the floor is a straight slope; beyond the first and last it is level. Type them as 0:1.0, 7:1.52, 10:2.0. Without them a pool is 1.0 m deep at the shallow end, 5 ft (1.52 m, the break point where the codes' slope limits change) at 60 % of its length and 2.0 m at the deep end. Each segment's slope is given as 1:n; Check Pools judges it against the codes.

Edges, coping and shell

  • Edge: skimmer pools have their water 0.15 m below the deck and a coping 0.3 m wide, overhanging the water by 40 mm (the MAHC's 1–2 in) and 60 mm thick. Deck-level gutter pools have their water at the deck and no coping.
  • Shell: 0.3 m of cast concrete by default, walls outside the outline and a base slab under the floor. The floor on the pool's level is cut round it.

The values are provisional (EXPERT_REVIEW.md E-116).

To change a pool, select it. Properties show and change:

  • its Deck Height and Water Level Below Deck;
  • its Shallow Depth and Deepest Depth, and its Cove Radius;
  • its Class;
  • the coping's width, overhang and thickness;
  • the shell's construction and thickness, its tile thickness (empty for none) and membrane.

Its entries are listed. The shell, water and openings follow each change. pool.set

Stairs, sloped entries, benches, recessed steps, beaches and swimouts

Select the pool and choose Interiors › Pools › Pool Entry, then click by the wall where the entry's middle goes. It runs from that edge into the pool and becomes part of it:

  • Stairs, 1.5 m wide: the depth at the wall split into equal risers no higher than 0.25 m, on 12 in (0.305 m) treads.
  • Sloped entry, 1.0 m wide: a 1:12 ramp from the deck to 27 in below the water, the ADA's accessible entry (§1009.3); it stops where the pool does.
  • Underwater bench, 2.0 m long and 0.45 m deep, its seat 0.45 m below the water.
  • Recessed steps, treads 18 in wide cut 5½ in into the wall, 10 in apart, the top one 10 in below the deck, down to 1.0 m under the water. A grab rail each side, 21 in apart and 0.85 m above the deck, curves over the coping into the water. The shell must leave 50 mm behind the treads.
  • Beach (zero-depth) entry: the floor rising 1:12 to the deck along the whole edge, or the width you type. The status bar says where it reaches the water and the floor.
  • Swimout: a seat 1.5 m wide and 18 in under the water in an alcove 24 in out behind the wall. The shell wraps round the alcove and the deck is cut round it.

Nosing stripes. Each stair tread and a swimout's seat get a 1½ in contrasting stripe along their front edge; type another width, or 0 for none. Check Pools holds stair stripes to the MAHC's and ISPSC's 1–2 in and a swimout to the ISPSC's 11 in depth, 240 in² seat, 20 in below the water and ¾–2 in stripe.

Handrails. Stairs and sloped entries get two handrails, one at each side, 36 in above the nosings or the ramp, of 1.90 in tube. Type how many (0 for none; more are spread across), their height, and the clear width between the outer two. Wide stairs need more than two: the MAHC wants them at most 12 ft apart.

The water's volume is less what they displace and more what recessed treads hold. Check Pools counts stairs, recessed steps and beaches as entries, a sloped entry as an accessible one, and measures treads, risers, widths, the ramp's slope and depth, benches, handrails (how many, how high, how far apart), recessed treads and the beach's slope. The sample A resort pool with a beach entry has one of each. From a script: pool.add_entry, pool.remove_entry, or pool.set with entries.

Competition lanes

The competition templates come with their lanes: six (eight for 50 m), the middle ones 2.5 m and the outer two taking what is left after 0.1 m at each side wall. Interiors › Pools › Pool Lanes changes their number and width, the side gaps, the touch pads (10 mm at each end that has them) and whether the markings are drawn: a line down each lane ending 2.0 m from the end walls in a 1.0 m cross, 0.5 m crosses 15 m from each end, and targets on the end walls.

Fittings in the same window add the lanes' equipment: lane ropes on the lines between the lanes (and outside the outer lanes where the side gap holds a whole float), their 0.15 m floats green outside, then blue, then yellow, red for the last 5 m at each end and at 15 m; backstroke flags on a rope 1.8 m over the water 5 m out from each end wall; and the false-start rope 1.2 m up, 15 m from the end races start from (the deeper, unless set). Each rope runs between posts on the deck 0.6 m back from the side walls. pool.lanes takes fittings, float, flag_height, flag_distance, false_start_height, false_start_distance and start, and Check Pools holds them to World Aquatics' FR 2.6, 2.9 and 2.11; a competition pool without them gets notes.

For a pool marked for competition, Check Pools applies World Aquatics: 50.000 or 25.000 m between the touch pads (+10 mm), lanes of 2.5 m (2.4 m outside), 1.0 m of water, 1.35 m from 1 to 6 m out where starting platforms stand, and their tops 0.50–0.75 m above the water. Its values are FINA's 2017–2021 rules with the 2026 checklist's changes, and each finding says to verify against the current World Aquatics text.

Diving boards

Place the Diving board 1 m, on stand from the Riviera Pools library on the deck, pointing over the water. Check Pools takes its plummet, the middle of its tip, and its height above the water, and checks the diving table's column for it: A back to the wall, B to the side walls, C to the next board's plummet, D to the wall ahead, E clear overhead, and the depths H at the plummet, K ahead and M to each side. Stairs or benches built into the pool inside the envelope are reported too. Public pools use the MAHC's Table 4.8.2.3; competition pools World Aquatics' springboard table. The sample A diving well has two boards that pass.

Equipment

The Riviera Pools library (Insert › Component Libraries) has a pool lift, a three-step ladder, a grab rail, a starting platform, a 1 m diving board, a lifeguard chair, main drain covers and an underwater light. Each faces the water at its front; a ladder's and a light's insertion point is the pool wall's face. Their clear decks show as virtual volumes and Check Rules reports what stands in them: the ADA's 36 × 48 in behind a lift, and the MAHC's 4 ft beside and behind diving boards, lifeguard chairs and lifts. Check Pools counts the ladders and lift as entries and the covers as drains, and does not count the equipment as blocking the deck.

Depth markings

Select a pool and choose Interiors › Pools › Pool Depth Markings. Markers go round its edge where the codes ask: at each end's and each side's least and greatest depth, at every slope change and at the 5 ft break, at every foot of depth up to 5 ft (the MAHC's step; set 2 ft for the ISPSC's, or 0.5 m), and more wherever two would be over 25 ft apart. Each reads the water's depth 3 ft out from the wall, to the nearest 3 in ("4 FT 6 IN") or to 0.1 m, or both; under the UK code they are in metres. Each has a tile on the deck within 18 in of the water and one on the wall just under the coping. Where the water is 5 ft deep or less, a NO DIVING tile stands beside it. A marker steps along the edge, clear of a ladder, platform or lift standing where it would go. In a pool whose floor passes 5 ft, a 4 in band marks the break on the floor and up the walls; the float line is placed 1 ft shallower. Check Pools checks the markings against the MAHC, the ISPSC and HSG179.

Barriers and gates

A pool needs a barrier round it: a fence, the walls of its hall or the house, with gates. Draw it with the railing tools (Architecture › Circulation › Railing, and Modify | Railings › Gate for its gates) and walls. View › Coordination › Pool Barriers shows what encloses the pool: Riviera fills the ground outward from the water until it meets railings, gates and walls, and lists those it meets with their heights, the gap under each railing and between its balusters, and which way each gate swings. If the ground reaches open space, it says the pool is not enclosed and where an opening let it through. Check Pools judges the barrier: the ISPSC asks for 48 in, at most 4 in under it, no 4 in opening (1¾ in where its rails are under 45 in apart) and gates opening outward, away from the pool; the MAHC asks for 6 ft round public pools. Doors and low windows in a house wall that is part of a home pool's barrier need alarms. A fence of the right height is a railing type: from a script, railing_type.create makes one (1.25 m with 2 in under it and 95 mm gaps meets the ISPSC).

Gate hardware. Select the gate (or its railing) and choose Modify | Railings › Gate Hardware: whether it closes, latches and locks itself, the latch's height and whether it is released outside, by a key or code, or from the pool side only. Check Pools holds it to the ISPSC (52–54 in at public pools, 54 in at least at homes, key latches 34–48 in, a pool-side latch at homes 3 in under the top with ½ in mesh round it) and the MAHC (54 in). A gate whose hardware is not set is a note.

Checking a pool

View › Coordination › Check Pools checks each pool against the pool rule packs in use. Under the IBC these are the ISPSC and the VGB Act's drain rules; with the ADA rule set, the ADA's §242 and §1009. The CDC's Model Aquatic Health Code is a project's choice: tick it in Manage › Model Properties under Pool rules. Each pack checks its own value, so where the codes disagree (1:12 or 1:10 above the break, a 4 ft or 6 ft deck) you see which pack a pool fails.

  • Class: a pool is public, competition, residential or wading, from its template or set with pool.set; each rule names the classes it applies to.
  • Slopes and depths: each segment either side of the 5 ft break, the shallow end, the deepest point, the cove and the coping.
  • Deck: a floor that reaches the width along every edge, with nothing standing in it.
  • Entries: Pool ladder, stairs and recessed steps within 1 m of the edge; the Pool lift, sloped entries and transfer walls as accessible means of entry. Under the MAHC and the ISPSC a beach entry counts too. A pool wall under 300 ft needs one, a lift or sloped entry; longer, two. The ladder, lift and drains are in the Riviera Pools library.
  • Drains: main drains in the pool, two at least 3 ft apart, or one with a cover larger than 18 × 23 in (unblockable).
  • Bather loads: the MAHC's 20 ft² of water a bather and the IBC's 50 ft² an occupant.

In England (the UK code setting) the packs are Sport England's design guidance (floor gradients of 1 in 20 under 0.8 m and 1 in 17 to 1.35 m, a 0.9 m shallow end, 2.0 m community lanes, a 2 m surround, steps with 0.14 m risers and 0.30 m goings, ramps of 1 in 15) and HSE's HSG179 (3 m² of water a bather). They are guidance, so their findings are notes. The sample A community pool in England meets them.

Findings marked secondary came from a checklist or summary, not the code's own text (the ISPSC's public-pool values): check them against your adopted edition.

The hall's air

View › Coordination › Pool Hall Air sets a pool's hall conditions (water and air temperatures, humidity, ASHRAE's activity factor, a cover, spectators) and works out what they ask:

  • the water evaporating, by ASHRAE's equation, in use, unoccupied and covered, and its latent load;
  • the water's heat losses: evaporation, convection and heating the make-up water;
  • outdoor air of 0.48 cfm/ft² of water and wet deck plus 7.5 cfm a spectator (ASHRAE 62.1, as the MAHC adopts it), a purge of twice that, and 5 % more exhaust to keep the hall negative;
  • the hall air's dew point (surfaces colder than it condense) and, with the outdoor design dew point, the outdoor air that would remove the evaporation alone.

The values are kept on the pool. The design loads give the space that holds it the pool's latent load.

Water treatment

View › Coordination › Pool Water Treatment sizes the plant from the pool's volume and type, on the MAHC's basis or (under the UK code) Sport England's and PWTAG's: the turnover (the MAHC's 6 h for most pools, 1 h for wading and 8 h for diving pools; Sport England's 2.5–4 h for swimming pools), the filtered flow, the filter area and its backwash, the surge or balance tank, the skimmers or gutters, the suction and discharge pipes' sizes, the flow each main drain carries, and the plant room's 15–30 % of the water area.

The plant room

Select the pool and choose Interiors › Pools › Pool Plant. Choose the basis, the number of filters and the pumps' head (15 m by default), then click inside the plant room. The plant Pool Water Treatment sizes goes along the room's longer side, 0.5 m off the wall with 0.8 m between items and 1.0 m in front:

  • a balance tank for the surge or balance volume, 1.5 m high;
  • a duty and a standby pump, each for the whole flow, the motor the next standard size above the flow times the head over 60 % efficiency;
  • the filters, vertical vessels 1.8 m high, each wide enough for its share of the filter area;
  • a hypochlorite and an acid tank in bunds, 2 m apart.

A room under 15 % of the water's area, or too small for the row, is noted. Clicking outside a room starts the row at the click. The items are tanks, pumps and filters you can schedule; placing the plant again replaces it. The pipes between them are not drawn yet. From a script: pool.plant.

Structure

View › Coordination › Pool Structure weighs the pool: its water at 9.81 kN/m³, its shell at 24.5 kN/m³ (concrete) and its tiles at 1.25 kN/m², in kN and per m² of footprint, on the floor at its deepest and along the walls. It gives the walls' pressure, shear and moment at the base as cantilevers of water. Give the water table's height above the pool's level, and it checks the empty pool's weight against the uplift, by 1.1 unless you say otherwise. Equipment by the pool (boards, lifts, chairs, platforms, ladders, rails) is listed with an advisory load for its anchorage. A pool with a level below it is taken as standing on a structure: you get the slab's span/360 deflection limit and the rooftop notes. From a script, pool.apply_loads puts the pool's weight on the slab's analytical panel as two area loads, the shell in one load case and the water in another.

Electrical zones and bonding

View › Coordination › Pool Electrical Zones measures each receptacle, switch, luminaire, board and pump round the pool from the water's edge and its height over the water. By default it applies NEC Article 680: receptacles 6 ft from the inside wall (GFCI within 20 ft), switches and disconnects 5 ft, luminaires within 5 ft at least 12 ft over the water (7 ft 6 in indoors with GFCI), and underwater lenses 18 in down. It sizes the bonding grid: 8 AWG copper on a 12 in grid under the 3 ft perimeter surface, tied to the shell's steel at four points. Under the UK, German or Australian code it applies IEC 60364-7-702's zones: zone 0 the water, zone 1 2.0 m out and 2.5 m up, zone 2 a further 1.5 m. Sockets and switches are kept out of zones 0 and 1, switchgear out of all three, and zone 0 takes 12 V SELV only. Both lists come from public summaries of the codes: check each finding against the edition that applies. The metal equipment to bond is listed too.

In IFC

A pool is an IfcElementAssembly (SWIMMINGPOOL) of an IfcWall, an IfcSlab (BASESLAB) and an IfcCovering (COPING), with its entries' IfcStair, IfcRamp and bench proxies and their handrails' IfcRailing (HANDRAIL). Its water is an IfcSpace (WATER) under the level, with its area and volume in Qto_SpaceBaseQuantities. No IFC version has a pool class, so this is Riviera's mapping.

Limits and what to check

  • Freeform outlines are typed, not yet sketched; profiles are changed from a script.
  • Gutters and infinity-edge basins are recorded but not drawn; the wall–floor cove is a value only; tiling and the membrane are not drawn.
  • Depth markers are tiles: their labels are listed, not yet drawn as text, and the NO DIVING symbol is a plain tile. The float line is placed but not drawn.
  • Barriers: the clear zone outside, the ground under a fence (grass or paving), double gates' secured leaf and the MAHC's exit gates are not modelled.
  • Not checked yet: where entries stand along the pool, 3 m boards and platforms, pool stairs' handrails under the ADA (20–24 in apart).
  • A beach's trench drain and the coping round a swimout are not built yet.
